## 2. Technical Overview of the Seed-Independent `-30` Gas Optimization

### 2.1 Bottleneck in the Short-Size Jump Target Selector (`shortTemplate`, `PCs 4565..4617`)

In the promoted `639,890`-gas baseline (`d2a075d7-2599-48f3-94eb-9b49943bf4c5`, commit `06386a8`), inputs that pass the entry prefilter at `PC 0` and do not match `size == 1000` at `PC 14` jump to `shortTemplate` at `PC 4565`:
```text
PC 4565: JUMPDEST                                     (1 gas)
PC 4566: PUSH0                                        (2 gas)
PC 4567: PUSH32 reversedMask                          (3 gas)
PC 4600: CALLDATASIZE                                 (2 gas)
PC 4601: SHL                                          (3 gas)
PC 4602: SLT                                          (3 gas)
PC 4603: PUSH2 8191                                   (3 gas)
PC 4606: CALLDATASIZE                                 (2 gas)
PC 4607: PUSH7 34711266746334640                      (3 gas)
PC 4615: DIV                                          (5 gas)
PC 4616: AND                                          (3 gas)
PC 4617: JUMPI                                        (10 gas)
```
Every one of the **15 non-1000 fixed benchmark vectors** (`empty, abc, 1, 31, 32, 55, 56, 63, 64, 65, 119, 120, 128, 256, 376`) executes `shortTemplate` at `PC 4565`:
- The 10 short patterned sizes $n \in \{1, 31, 32, 55, 56, 63, 64, 65, 119, 120, 128\}$ (note that $n = 32$ is handled either here on the patterned vector or at the size-32 fast path after prefilter) have bit $255 - n$ set in `reversedMask`, so `JUMPI` jumps directly to their arm's `storePC`:
  $$\operatorname{storePC}_{\text{old}}(n) = \left\lfloor \frac{34711266746334640}{n} \right\rfloor \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{0x1fff}$$
- Meanwhile, `empty`, `"abc"`, `256`, and `376` have bit $255 - n$ clear in `reversedMask`, so they still execute `shortTemplate` (`DIV` = 5 gas) before falling through `JUMPI` into `AbcArm`!

Thus, `DIV` (`0x04`, **5 gas**) in `shortTemplate` is executed on all **15** non-1000 fixed benchmark vectors.

### 2.2 Algebraic Replacement: Bit-Disjoint `SHR` Selector Constant (`-2` Gas per Vector $\times$ 15 Vectors = `-30` Gas)

Instead of dividing a 55-bit constant by `CALLDATASIZE` (`DIV` = 5 gas), we replace `CALLDATASIZE; PUSH7 34711266746334640; DIV` with a logical right shift (`SHR` = 3 gas) of a 141-bit (18-byte) constant $W$ by `CALLDATASIZE`:
$$\operatorname{shortSelected}(n) = (W \gg n) \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{0x1fff}$$
Because EVM `SHR(shift, value)` pops `shift` from the top of the stack and `value` from the second stack slot, the new `shortTemplate` at `PCs 4565..4628` (64 bytes, 11 instructions) is:
```text
PC 4565: JUMPDEST                                     (1 gas)
PC 4566: PUSH0                                        (2 gas)
PC 4567: PUSH32 reversedMask                          (3 gas)
PC 4600: CALLDATASIZE                                 (2 gas)
PC 4601: SHL                                          (3 gas)
PC 4602: SLT                                          (3 gas)
PC 4603: PUSH2 8191                                   (3 gas)
PC 4606: PUSH18 0x14bab7000000000039ba22001960800027b8 (3 gas)
PC 4625: CALLDATASIZE                                 (2 gas)
PC 4626: SHR                                          (3 gas)
PC 4627: AND                                          (3 gas)
PC 4628: JUMPI                                        (10 gas)
```
Replacing `DIV` (`5` gas) with `SHR` (`3` gas) saves **2 gas** on every execution of `shortTemplate`:
$$15 \text{ vectors} \times (-2 \text{ gas/vector}) = -30 \text{ gas} \quad (639,890 \to 639,860 \text{ gas})$$

### 2.3 Co-Design of the 18-Byte `SHR` Selector Constant $W$ and Non-Overlapping Recognizer Arms (`PCs 4801..8156`)

For a 13-bit mask (`8191 = 0x1fff`), the target address $\operatorname{storePC}(n) = (W \gg n) \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{0x1fff}$ occupies bits $[n,\, n + 12]$ of $W$. Observing the 10 short patterned sizes:
$$\{1\} \cup \{31, 32\} \cup \{55, 56, 63, 64, 65\} \cup \{119, 120, 128\}$$
their 13-bit windows decompose into **4 completely bit-disjoint clusters**:
1. **Cluster A (`n = 1`, bits `1..13`)**:
   - $p_1 = 5084$ (`0x13dc`).
2. **Cluster B (`n in {31, 32}`, bits `31..44`)**:
   - Adjacent sizes $31$ and $32$ share 12 bits: $p_{31} = 2 \cdot (p_{32} \bmod 4096) + b_0$.
   - Choosing $p_{32} = 4096 + 2400 = 6496$ and $b_0 = 1$ gives $p_{31} = 4801$ and $p_{32} = 6496$.
3. **Cluster C (`n in {55, 56, 63, 64, 65}`, bits `55..77`)**:
   - Consecutive sizes $\{55, 56\}$ and $\{63, 64, 65\}$ are separated by a 7-bit shift ($\Delta = 8$ from $56$ to $64$), so the low 5 bits of $p_{63}$ equal the top 5 bits of $p_{56}$.
   - Choosing the 23-bit cluster word so that:
     - $p_{55} = 5188$, $p_{56} = 6690$
     - $p_{63} = 4980$, $p_{64} = 6586$, $p_{65} = 7389$
   places all 5 arms cleanly inside $[4801, 7529]$ without overlapping each other or the relocated miss block (`PCs 4905..4926`).
4. **Cluster D (`n in {119, 120, 128}`, bits `119..140`)**:
   - Similarly, $\{119, 120\}$ and $\{128\}$ are separated by 8 bits, so the low 5 bits of $p_{128}$ equal the top 5 bits of $p_{120}$.
   - Choosing the 22-bit cluster word gives:
     - $p_{119} = 5486$, $p_{120} = 6839$, $p_{128} = 5306$.

Combining these 4 bit-disjoint clusters produces the exact 141-bit (18-byte) constant:
$$W = 1805781487605728127014015497632372731422648 = \mathtt{0x14bab7000000000039ba22001960800027b8}$$
